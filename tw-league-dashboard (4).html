Draft Recaps 2025? How Value is calculated
Value = a player's actual season points minus the points scored by whoever finished at the position rank matching where they were drafted.

Positions are ranked separately from each other (WR vs. WR, RB vs. RB, etc.) using both draft order and final season points.
Ex: Drafted as the 4th RB off the board, but finished as the actual #1 RB (416.6 pts). The real RB4 finisher scored 366.3 pts.
Value = 416.6 − 366.3 = +50.3

Pos rank and draft pick are both position-relative (e.g. WR4 = 4th-best scoring WR). Value compares actual points to what a typical player taken at that draft slot scored.

Manager Efficiency ? How Efficiency is calculated
Each week, we compare the points a manager's starting lineup actually scored against the highest-scoring lineup they COULD have started, given their full roster (starters + bench) and the league's position requirements.

Efficiency = Actual Points ÷ Optimal Points, averaged across every week.
100% would mean a manager started their best possible lineup every single week — no player left on the bench who outscored a starter.

Ranked by efficiency — how close each manager came to their maximum possible score every week, not by who won the most.

01Player Duos

How duos are picked
  1. Every manager is paired with the player who scored the most starter points for them across 2024 and 2025 combined.
  2. Only points from weeks the player was in that manager's starting lineup count. Bench weeks and points scored for other managers do not.
  3. The personality tag comes from the player's position and uses the same names as the Awards tab.

Every manager and the one player who defines their run, the player who scored the most for them from a starting lineup.

01Trade Leaderboard

How trade value is calculated
  1. A trade is two managers swapping players in the same week. Each side is the group of players it received.
  2. A side's value is the starter points those players scored from the trade week through Week 17. A week counts only if the player was in a starting lineup, for any manager. That includes weeks after he was dropped or traded away. Bench weeks count as zero.
  3. The winner is the side with more points. The margin is the gap between the two sides.
  4. A flip is its own trade. If you trade a player away later, that new trade is judged separately, and nothing is credited back to the first trade.
  5. Leaderboard net points add up the margins from every trade a manager made, positive for wins and negative for losses.

Trades are rebuilt from weekly roster changes, so trades with only draft picks, and some one sided moves, are not included. It is hindsight scoring, so it shows who came out ahead, not who was right at the time.

Net points won across every trade. Each side is credited with the starter points its received players scored from the trade week through week 17. A player's points count in any week he was in any manager's starting lineup, even after he was dropped or traded away. A flip counts as its own trade.
